01 · What is a Determinant?

A determinant is a scalar value associated with a square matrix. It is written using vertical bars.

Determinant of order 1

|a| = a

Determinant of order 2

|a b; c d| = ad − bc

Only square matrices have determinants.

The determinant helps determine whether a square matrix is invertible and is used in solving systems of linear equations.

02 · Determinant of a 3 × 3 Matrix

For a 3 × 3 matrix, expand along a row or a column using the corresponding signs.

|a b c; d e f; g h i|
= a(ei − fh) − b(di − fg) + c(dh − eg)

Sign pattern

+   −   +
−   +   −
+   −   +

Memorise this pattern for cofactor expansion.

03 · Minors and Cofactors

Minor

The minor Mᵢⱼ is the determinant obtained after deleting row i and column j.

Mᵢⱼ = determinant after deletion

Cofactor

The cofactor includes the sign determined by the element's position.

Cᵢⱼ = (−1)ⁱ⁺ʲ Mᵢⱼ
Remember: Cofactors in the first row have signs +, −, +. Do not forget the negative sign in the middle term when expanding along the first row.

04 · Important Properties

PropertyResult
Interchange two rows or columnsDeterminant changes sign.
Two rows or columns are identicalDeterminant is zero.
A row or column is entirely zeroDeterminant is zero.
Multiply one row by kDeterminant is multiplied by k.
Add a multiple of one row to anotherDeterminant remains unchanged.
Transpose a matrixdet(Aᵀ) = det(A).
Product of square matricesdet(AB) = det(A)det(B).
Common trap: If every element of a row is multiplied by k, the determinant is multiplied by k—not by k for every element separately.

05 · Area of a Triangle

For points (x₁,y₁), (x₂,y₂), and (x₃,y₃), the area is:

Area = ½ |x₁(y₂ − y₃) + x₂(y₃ − y₁) + x₃(y₁ − y₂)|

If the area is zero, the three points are collinear.

Use the absolute value because geometric area cannot be negative.

06 · Adjoint and Inverse

The adjoint of a square matrix is the transpose of its cofactor matrix.

adj(A) = (cofactor matrix of A)ᵀ

If det(A) ≠ 0, then A is invertible and:

A⁻¹ = adj(A) / det(A)
For a 2 × 2 matrix:
A = [[a,b],[c,d]]
adj(A) = [[d,−b],[−c,a]]

When det(A) = 0, the inverse does not exist.

07 · Solving Linear Equations

For a system AX = B, if A is invertible, then:

X = A⁻¹B

This provides a matrix method for solving a compatible system of linear equations.

Example

x + y = 5
2x + 3y = 12

From the first equation, x = 5 − y. Substituting into the second:

2(5 − y) + 3y = 12

10 + y = 12 → y = 2 → x = 3

Answer: x = 3, y = 2

09 · Solved Examples

Example 1 — Determinant of order 2

A = [[3,2],[1,4]]

det(A) = 3×4 − 2×1

det(A) = 10

Example 2 — Determinant of order 3

A = [[1,2,3],[0,1,4],[5,6,0]]

Expand along the first row:

det(A) = 1(1×0 − 4×6) − 2(0×0 − 4×5) + 3(0×6 − 1×5)
= −24 + 40 − 15
det(A) = 1

Example 3 — Inverse exists or not?

A = [[2,4],[1,2]]

det(A) = 2×2 − 4×1 = 0

The inverse does not exist.

Example 4 — Area of a triangle

Find the area of the triangle with vertices (0,0), (4,0), and (0,3).

Area = ½ × |0(0−3) + 4(3−0) + 0(0−0)|
= ½ × 12 = 6
Answer: 6 square units

10 · Board Practice

Try solving each question before revealing the answer.

Q1. Evaluate |3 2; 1 4|.

3×4 − 2×1 = 10.

Q2. What happens to a determinant if two rows are interchanged?

Its sign changes.

Q3. Find the determinant of [[1,2],[2,4]].

1×4 − 2×2 = 0.

Q4. State the condition for a square matrix to have an inverse.

Its determinant must be nonzero.

Q5. Find the determinant of [[1,2,3],[0,1,4],[5,6,0]].

Expanding along the first row gives −24 + 40 − 15 = 1.

Q6. What is the area of a triangle with vertices (0,0), (4,0), (0,3)?

Area = ½ × base × height = ½ × 4 × 3 = 6 square units.

12 · Quick Revision

Must remember

  • For a 2 × 2 determinant: ad − bc.
  • Interchanging two rows changes the sign.
  • Identical rows make the determinant zero.
  • det(Aᵀ) = det(A).
  • det(AB) = det(A)det(B) for square matrices.
  • A⁻¹ = adj(A)/det(A) when det(A) ≠ 0.

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting the alternating cofactor signs.
  • Confusing a minor with a cofactor.
  • Forgetting the absolute value in area calculations.
  • Finding an inverse without checking the determinant.
