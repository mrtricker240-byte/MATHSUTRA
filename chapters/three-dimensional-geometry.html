CLASS 12 MATHEMATICS · CHAPTER 11

Three-Dimensional Geometry

Understand direction cosines, direction ratios, equations of lines in 3D, angles and distances.

Language: English · Mode: Boards

1. Coordinates in Three Dimensions

A point in 3D is represented by P(x,y,z). The three coordinate axes are mutually perpendicular.

The distance between P(x₁,y₁,z₁) and Q(x₂,y₂,z₂) is:

PQ = √[(x₂−x₁)² + (y₂−y₁)² + (z₂−z₁)²]

Example

Find the distance between A(1,2,3) and B(4,6,3).

AB = √[(4−1)²+(6−2)²+(3−3)²] = 5

2. Direction Ratios (DRs)

Direction ratios are any three numbers proportional to the components that describe a line's direction.

If a line has direction ratios a, b, c, its direction vector can be written as:

d⃗ = aî + bĵ + ck̂

Direction ratios are not unique: multiplying all three by the same nonzero constant gives the same direction.

3. Direction Cosines (DCs)

If a line makes angles α, β and γ with the positive x, y and z axes, respectively:

l = cos α, m = cos β, n = cos γ
l² + m² + n² = 1

If the direction ratios are a, b, c, then:

l = a/√(a²+b²+c²)
m = b/√(a²+b²+c²)
n = c/√(a²+b²+c²)

4. Vector Equation of a Line

A line passing through a point with position vector a⃗ and parallel to a nonzero vector d⃗ has equation:

r⃗ = a⃗ + λd⃗

Here λ is a real parameter.

If the line passes through A(x₁,y₁,z₁) and has direction ratios a, b, c:

r⃗ = (x₁î+y₁ĵ+z₁k̂) + λ(aî+bĵ+ck̂)

5. Cartesian Equation of a Line

For a line through (x₁,y₁,z₁) with direction ratios a, b, c:

(x−x₁)/a = (y−y₁)/b = (z−z₁)/c

This symmetric form assumes a, b and c are all nonzero. If a direction ratio is zero, use the corresponding coordinate as a constant and write the other relations separately.

Example

Line through (1,2,3) with direction ratios (2,−1,4):

(x−1)/2 = (y−2)/(−1) = (z−3)/4

6. Line Through Two Points

For A(x₁,y₁,z₁) and B(x₂,y₂,z₂), direction ratios are:

(x₂−x₁, y₂−y₁, z₂−z₁)

The vector equation of AB is:

r⃗ = a⃗ + λ(b⃗−a⃗)

For the line segment AB itself, 0 ≤ λ ≤ 1. For the entire line, λ can be any real number.

7. Angle Between Two Lines

If the lines have direction ratios (a₁,b₁,c₁) and (a₂,b₂,c₂), the acute or smaller angle θ between the lines satisfies:

cos θ = |a₁a₂+b₁b₂+c₁c₂| / [√(a₁²+b₁²+c₁²) √(a₂²+b₂²+c₂²)]

For directed vectors, the absolute value may be omitted when calculating the angle between the vectors themselves.

Perpendicular lines

a₁a₂ + b₁b₂ + c₁c₂ = 0

8. Parallel Lines

Two lines are parallel when their direction ratios are proportional.

a₁/a₂ = b₁/b₂ = c₁/c₂

When denominators are zero, compare the direction vectors by checking whether one is a scalar multiple of the other.

9. Skew Lines

Skew lines are lines that do not intersect and are not parallel. They occur in three-dimensional geometry, not in a single plane.

To investigate two lines, check whether their direction vectors are proportional and whether the lines intersect.

10. Shortest Distance Between Skew Lines

For lines:

r⃗ = a⃗ + λb⃗
r⃗ = c⃗ + μd⃗

where b⃗ and d⃗ are nonparallel direction vectors, the shortest distance is:

SD = |(c⃗−a⃗) · (b⃗×d⃗)| / |b⃗×d⃗|

The numerator measures the magnitude of the scalar triple product; the denominator gives the magnitude of the perpendicular direction.

11. Formula Vault

Distance = √[(Δx)²+(Δy)²+(Δz)²]
l² + m² + n² = 1
r⃗ = a⃗ + λd⃗
(x−x₁)/a = (y−y₁)/b = (z−z₁)/c
cos θ = |d⃗₁ · d⃗₂|/(|d⃗₁||d⃗₂|)
SD = |(c⃗−a⃗) · (b⃗×d⃗)|/|b⃗×d⃗|

🧠 Solved Examples

Example 1 — Direction Cosines

Find the direction cosines of a line with direction ratios 2, 3, 6.

√(2²+3²+6²) = 7
l = 2/7, m = 3/7, n = 6/7

Example 2 — Equation of a Line

Find the Cartesian equation of the line through (2,−1,3) with direction ratios (1,2,−2).

(x−2)/1 = (y+1)/2 = (z−3)/(−2)

Example 3 — Angle Between Lines

Find the angle between direction vectors (1,0,0) and (0,1,0).

Dot product = 0
cos θ = 0 → θ = 90°

Example 4 — Distance Between Points

Find the distance between A(1,2,3) and B(4,6,3).

AB = √(9+16+0) = 5

Example 5 — Shortest Distance Between Skew Lines

Consider r⃗ = λî and r⃗ = ĵ + μk̂.

The direction vectors are b⃗ = î and d⃗ = k̂. The displacement between the base points is c⃗−a⃗ = ĵ.

b⃗ × d⃗ = î × k̂ = −ĵ
SD = |ĵ · (−ĵ)|/|−ĵ| = 1

📝 CBSE Board Practice

1. Find the distance between A(1,0,2) and B(4,4,2).

√(3²+4²+0²) = 5.

2. Find the direction cosines corresponding to direction ratios 1, 2, 2.

Magnitude = 3. Direction cosines are 1/3, 2/3, 2/3.

3. Write the Cartesian equation of the line through (1,2,3) with direction ratios (2,1,−1).

(x−1)/2 = (y−2)/1 = (z−3)/(−1).

4. Find the angle between direction vectors (1,0,0) and (1,1,0).

cos θ = 1/√2, so θ = 45°.

5. State the condition for two lines with direction vectors a⃗ and b⃗ to be perpendicular.

For nonzero direction vectors, a⃗ · b⃗ = 0.

📌 Final Revision

  • Direction ratios describe the direction of a line.
  • Direction cosines satisfy l² + m² + n² = 1.
  • A line can be expressed in vector or Cartesian form.
  • Parallel lines have proportional direction vectors.
  • Perpendicular directions have zero dot product.
  • Skew lines are nonparallel and nonintersecting.
